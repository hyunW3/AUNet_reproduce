3 throughput = text-bytes per wall-second (bytes ÷ (parse·KB + forward)) — the fair cross-tokenizer axis. Byte models: bsz4·8192 B = 32 KB/step. 4 Llama: seq 2048 tok × 4.541 B/tok × bsz4 = 36.3 KB/step, parse+fwd 53.8 ms → ~675 KB/s (forward-only ~748) — fastest, subword packs 4.541 B/tok so its 2048-tok window covers ≈9.3 KB text (vs byte 8 KB); not iso-FLOP. 5 BLT forward = 58.9 base + 79.2 entropy = 138.1 ms (fp32 old = 451.9).

Read: Llama leads downstream (47.4 avg3) and throughput (~675 KB/s vs byte ~495–509) — subword packs 4.541 B/tok, so it clears more text per step, though at a different (non-iso-FLOP) compute-per-byte axis. Among byte models rg ≈ AU-Net ≈ ByteFlow; BLT is ~2× slower (entropy model in the loop). ByteFlow global-top-k is weakest on both BPB and downstream.

Llama's EM collapses to 0 on all three (subword re-tokenizes the despaced haystack); a softer "contains-target" metric (*_tf) stays high for all (S3 Llama 0→95.3, rg 46→98.2) — despace breaks exact formatting more than retrieval. Read: BPEByte-rg is strongest on every clean probe — perfect S-NIAH-3 UUID copy (1.00 vs Llama 0.16, AU-Net 0.52), best sparse FFLM (0.869 vs AU-Net 0.666), best Dyck-3 (0.673), best MK-NIAH at every K (K=8: 0.55 vs Llama 0.175). AU-Net (static word-pooling) sits between; Llama floors on exact-copy of unseen character strings — byte-level character addressing is the lever. Under despace the exact-match story is harsher for everyone (Llama →0) and the winner is task-dependent (AU-Net on S1, rg on S2/S3).
