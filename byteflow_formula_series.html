ByteFlow Coding-Rate Chunking — Formula Series

Exact lossy coding rate (eqs 11–12), its two approximations, the differences between them, and a retrospective on why logdet_quantile was used first. Source: ByteFlow: Language Modeling through Adaptive Byte Compression without a Tokenizer (arXiv 2603.03583), §3 + Appendices A/B.

0. Setup & notation

The local encoder produces contextualized byte representations

$$ h_{1:t} \in \mathbb{R}^{t \times d}, \qquad d \equiv d_{\text{local}}, $$

with rows $h_1,\dots,h_t$, each $h_s\in\mathbb{R}^{d}$. Symbols used throughout:

  • $\varepsilon^2$ — noise-variance parameter (sensitivity of the coding rate).
  • $\alpha \equiv \dfrac{d}{\varepsilon^2}$ — scale factor.
  • $G_t \equiv \displaystyle\sum_{s\le t} h_s h_s^{\top} \in \mathbb{R}^{d\times d}$ — un-normalized Gram / second-moment matrix (diagonal = per-feature energy; off-diagonals = cross-feature correlations).
  • $M_t \equiv I_d + \alpha\,G_t$ — regularized second-moment matrix; $M_t = M_{t-1} + \alpha\, h_t h_t^{\top}$ (rank-1 update).

Selection (all modes): a position is a chunk boundary when its marginal coding rate $\Delta R_t$ is high. The _quantile suffix means selection is a causal threshold $\tau$ tracked as an EMA of the batch $(1-p)$ quantile, $p = 1/\text{target\_ratio}$ — identical across the three variants below. Only the score $\Delta R_t$ differs.

1. The target — exact lossy coding rate

Coding rate of a prefix (eq 11):

$$ R_\varepsilon(h_{1:t}) \;=\; \tfrac{1}{2}\,\log\det\!\Big(I_{d} + \tfrac{d}{\varepsilon^{2}}\, h_{1:t}^{\top} h_{1:t}\Big) \;=\; \tfrac12 \log\det\!\big(I_d + \alpha\,G_t\big) \;=\; \tfrac12\log\det M_t. $$

(Eq 11 is written with the $t\times t$ form $\det\!\big(I_t+\alpha\,h_{1:t}h_{1:t}^{\top}\big)$; by Sylvester's identity $\det(I_t+AB)=\det(I_d+BA)$ it equals the $d\times d$ form above, which is tractable since $d\ll t$.)

Marginal coding rate at position $t$ (eq 12):

$$ \Delta R_t \;=\; R_\varepsilon(h_{1:t}) - R_\varepsilon(h_{1:t-1}). $$

Intuition: $R_\varepsilon$ is large when $h_{1:t}$ spans many diverse directions; $\Delta R_t$ is the information gained by adding byte $t$ — large $\Delta R_t \Rightarrow$ natural segmentation boundary.

2. The three implementations

exact_quantile — paper eqs 11–12

Full log-det, full prefix

Compute $\Delta R_t$ exactly via the matrix-determinant lemma $\det(M_{t-1}+\alpha h_t h_t^{\top}) = \det(M_{t-1})\,(1+\alpha\,h_t^{\top}M_{t-1}^{-1}h_t)$:

$$ \boxed{\;\Delta R_t \;=\; \tfrac12\,\log\!\Big(1 + \alpha\, h_t^{\top} M_{t-1}^{-1} h_t\Big)\;} \qquad M_{t-1} = I_d + \alpha\,G_{t-1}. $$

Streamed by a Sherman–Morrison rank-1 inverse scan maintaining $P_t \equiv M_t^{-1}$, $P_0=I_d$:

$$ v_t = P_{t-1} h_t,\quad q_t = h_t^{\top} v_t,\quad \Delta R_t = \tfrac12\log(1+\alpha q_t),\quad P_t = P_{t-1} - \frac{\alpha\, v_t v_t^{\top}}{1+\alpha q_t}. $$

Keeps the full $d\times d$ $M^{-1}$ (off-diagonals included) over the full prefix $[1{:}t]$ — so it measures how many independent directions the representation already occupies (is $h_t$ a genuinely new direction, or one already used?). Causal. Cost $O(T d^2)$, sequential in $t$ (~6× slower). Verified against brute-force logdet to $3\times10^{-5}$.

logdet_quantile — non-paper variant

Diagonal log-det, trailing window

Two simplifications of §2-exact: (i) keep only the diagonal of the Gram (drop off-diagonal cross-feature terms ⇒ a sum of independent per-feature scalars); (ii) replace the full prefix with a trailing boxcar window of $W$ bytes.

$$ \Delta R_t \;\approx\; \tfrac12\sum_{f=1}^{d}\log\!\Big(1 + \frac{\alpha\, h_{t,f}^{2}}{1 + \alpha\, g_{t-1,f}}\Big), \qquad g_{t-1,f} = \!\!\sum_{t-W \le s < t}\!\! h_{s,f}^{2}. $$

Equivalent to replacing $M_{t-1}^{-1}$ in the exact form by the diagonal $\operatorname{diag}\!\big(1+\alpha\,g_{t-1,f}\big)^{-1}$. Fully vectorized, $O(T d)$. The window $W$ was added to keep the score stationary (the cumulative prefix energy grows without bound, making $\Delta R_t$ decay with $t$). Cannot see directional redundancy across features — only per-feature energy novelty.

l2_quantile — paper eq 29 (Appendix B)

L2 / Frobenius approximation

First-order matrix-log expansion $\log\det(I+A)=\operatorname{tr}\log(I+A)\approx\operatorname{tr}(A)$ applied to $A=\alpha\,h_{1:t}^{\top}h_{1:t}$, with $\operatorname{tr}(h_{1:t}^{\top}h_{1:t})=\|h_{1:t}\|_F^2$:

$$ R_\varepsilon(h_{1:t}) \;\approx\; \tfrac{\alpha}{2}\,\|h_{1:t}\|_F^{2} \;=\; \tfrac{\alpha}{2}\sum_{s\le t}\|h_s\|_2^2 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \boxed{\;\Delta R_t \;\propto\; \|h_t\|_2^{2}\;} $$

The marginal collapses to the per-token squared norm — equivalent to replacing $M_{t-1}^{-1}$ by $\alpha I$ (drop the denominator entirely). $O(T d)$, vectorized. This is the paper's stated streaming approximation. Implementation note: $\|h_t\|^2$ has a non-stationary scale (representation norms grow during training), so in our leak-free threshold setting it is normalized by its causal trailing-window mean before thresholding; the paper's rank-based Top-K is scale-free and needs no such normalization.

3. How the implementation works — end-to-end

The score $\Delta R_t$ from §2 is only step 3 of a longer pipeline. Here is how raw bytes become a prediction, and where coding-rate chunking plugs into the AU-Net backbone. Code: lingua/apps/aunet/coding_rate.py (the chunker) and hierarchical.py (the model hook).

3.1 Where it sits — the AU-Net hierarchy

ByteFlow keeps the AU-Net U-shape unchanged; chunking only decides which byte positions are promoted into the deep trunk. A 2-stage (single-level) hierarchy, the only shape used here:

bytes x₁:ₜ ∈ V^T (V = 256 byte vocab, T = 8192) │ tok_embeddings ▼ Local Encoder (3 layers, dim 512, sliding-window attn) │ ▼ h₁:ₜ ∈ ℝ^{T×512} ◀── contextualized byte reps │ ┌────┴───────────────────────────────────────────┐ │ CODING-RATE CHUNKING (the new part, §2–§3.3) │ │ score ΔRₜ → causal threshold → level_mask │ └────┬───────────────────────────────────────────┘ │ down: gather the K promoted rows, project 512→768 ▼ z₁:ₖ ∈ ℝ^{K×768} (K ≪ T, K ≈ T / 4.5) Global Trunk (10 layers, dim 768, full causal attn) ◀── most of the FLOPs live here │ ▼ g₁:ₖ │ up: scatter back to T, sₜ = hₜ + s̃ₜ (residual + 16-bin position map) ▼ Decoder (3 layers, dim 512) │ vocab head ▼ p̂(xₜ₊₁ | x₁:ₜ) ∈ Δ^{256}

(Dims shown are the 100M config [512,768] / [3,10]. The trunk runs on $K\!\approx\!T/4.5$ tokens instead of $T$, which is why compressing well matters: it sets the trunk's compute budget.)

3.2 The one structural change: boundaries move into forward()

AU-Net's defining rule is that the model never computes boundaries — whitespace / BPEByte produce an integer level_mask in the data loader, and the model just consumes it. Coding-rate chunking cannot do that, because $\Delta R_t$ is a function of the learned encoder output $h$, which only exists after encoders[0] runs. So the boundary producer moves into the forward pass — the only real change to the model:

# hierarchical.py — HierarchicalTransformer.forward (coding-rate path) x = self.tok_embeddings(token_values) enc0_out = None if self.coding_rate is not None: # run encoder up front: h needed for boundaries enc0_out = self.encoders[0](x, attn_impl="fmha") level_mask = self.coding_rate(enc0_out, nb_levels=1) # ◀── model-side level_mask masks, _, nb_toks = self.get_pool_mask(level_mask, [...], force_first=True) for i, (encoder, trans, mask) in enumerate(...): x = enc0_out if (i==0 and enc0_out is not None) else encoder(x) # reuse, no recompute residuals.append(x); x = trans.down(x, mask, ...) # pool to K rows x = self.trunk(x) ... trans.up(...) ... decoder(x) ... logits

For whitespace / BPEByte, self.coding_rate is None and the data-supplied level_mask is used verbatim — so all three boundary schemes share one backbone, isolating the comparison to the boundary rule alone.

3.3 The chunker — score → select → mask

CodingRateChunker(h, nb_levels) turns $h\in\mathbb{R}^{B\times T\times d}$ into an integer level_mask$\in\{0,1\}^{B\times T}$ (1 = boundary). Three internal stages:

1
Score. Compute $\Delta R_t$ per position by one of the §2 formulas (exact Sherman–Morrison scan / logdet diagonal-window / l2 squared norm). All causal: $\Delta R_t$ uses only $h_{1:t}$.
2
Threshold. A position is a boundary iff $\;\text{score}_t \ge \tau$. $\tau$ is a single scalar tracked as an EMA of the per-batch $(1-p)$ quantile, $p=1/\text{target\_ratio}$ (§3.4). The decision uses the pre-update $\tau$ — never the current sequence's own future.
3
Emit. level_mask = (score ≥ τ).long(), with position 0 forced to a boundary (BOS). Capping to the trunk's max length is handled downstream by get_pool_mask.
# coding_rate.py — CodingRateChunker.forward (@torch.no_grad) score = self._score(h) # §2: exact | logdet | l2 if self.mode.endswith("fixed_tau"): boundary = score >= self.tau_buf elif self.mode.endswith("quantile"): boundary = score >= self._ema_tau(score) # causal threshold elif self.mode == "rolling_topk": boundary = self._rolling_threshold(score) # causal, trailing-window else: boundary = self._global_topk(score) # NON-causal (diagnostic) level_mask = boundary.long() * nb_levels level_mask[:, 0] = nb_levels # always keep BOS return level_mask

3.4 Calibrating & keeping it leak-free

Why a threshold, not Top-K. The paper uses global Top-K, which ranks all $T$ positions together — including future bytes — so a position's promotion depends on tokens after it. For an autoregressive byte LM that leaks the future into the trunk input. A causal threshold (decide each $t$ from its prefix only) is leak-free by construction and identical at train / eval / generation. Verified: perturbing future bytes leaves every boundary in $[0,t)$ unchanged for the threshold modes, and changes them for global_topk (the intended leak).

Hitting the target ratio. $\tau$ is tracked as a direct EMA of the batch $(1-p)$ quantile, so the boundary rate self-calibrates to $p=1/\text{target\_ratio}$ (≈ 4.5 bytes/chunk, matching BPEByte for iso-FLOPs).

The scale-invariance fix. $\|h_t\|^2$ has a non-stationary scale — representation norms grow during training — so a global threshold drifts and chunking collapses (we saw nbtoks fall to ~100, ratio ~1800). Top-K is immune because it is rank-based; a threshold is not. Fix: the l2 score is normalized by its causal trailing-window mean, $\;\text{score}_t = \|h_t\|^2 / \tfrac1W\!\sum_{t-W\le s\le t}\|h_s\|^2$, making it stationary so the EMA threshold stays calibrated (verified ratio 4.25–4.77 under deliberately growing norms). The exact/logdet log-det scores are already bounded, so they never collapsed.

3.5 From mask to compute — pooling & upsampling

get_pool_mask turns level_mask > i into a per-level keep-mask (capped to the trunk's max_seqlen = 3200). Then the existing AU-Net transition does the resampling, unchanged:

  • down — gather only the $K$ promoted rows of $h$ and project $512\!\to\!768$ → $z_{1:K}$. Gradients reach the encoder through this gather (the mask itself is discrete / detached, like AU-Net's existing pooling).
  • trunk — full causal attention over the $K$ compressed tokens.
  • up — scatter $g_{1:K}$ back to all $T$ positions via a byte→chunk index, add a 16-bin position map, and a large residual from the encoder: $\;s_t = h_t + \tilde s_t$. (Matches the paper's eqs 14–17, $B{=}16$ bins.)

3.6 Train / eval / generation parity

Because the threshold is causal and prefix-deterministic, the same boundaries are produced in all three regimes from a single code path — no separate eval-boundary logic (unlike BPEByte, which needs a dedicated prefill path). At generation, the chunker streams: maintain the running score state and the scalar $\tau$, and commit a boundary the instant $\text{score}_t \ge \tau$ — no rollback, no future dependence. (The Top-K family is the exception: it is not prefix-deterministic, so it would need the two-mode eval split — global vs rolling — described in the plan.)

Reading the rightmost-but-one column top to bottom shows the progressive simplification: full inverse → diagonal inverse → scaled identity. Each step throws away more of the correlation structure the exact log-det captures.

Empirical result (100M, matched backbone/budget, 1672 steps). Final per-byte CE (≈ BPB): logdet 0.997, l2 1.007, exact 1.027 (rank-0; cross-rank means ≈ 0.99–1.005) — statistically tied. So the off-diagonal / full-prefix information the exact form preserves did not help here, and all three sit ~0.07 BPB behind the leak-free BPEByte peer (v4_root_greedy 0.931). The cheap L2 form is therefore the sensible default; the gap to BPEByte is not a score-fidelity problem.

5. Why logdet_quantile was used first

It was an over-engineered intermediate chosen before the paper's exact equations were locked down — a methodological misstep, owned here:

  1. The PDF hadn't parsed yet. At first implementation only a lossy web extraction was available. It gave the exact log-det (eq 11) but rendered the Appendix-B approximation vaguely as “$R_\varepsilon \propto \|h_{1:t}\|^2$,” without the derivation (eqs 26–29) that collapses the marginal to the bare per-token norm $\Delta R_t \propto \|h_t\|^2$.
  2. The bare L2 looked “too trivial.” A per-token norm seemed to discard the cumulative/log-det structure that is the method's whole point, so it didn't look trustworthy as the faithful form.
  3. A “middle ground” was invented. The diagonal log-det keeps the per-feature $\log(1+\cdot)$ shape (more log-det-like than a bare norm) while staying $O(Td)$ and vectorizable; the trailing window was added to cure the non-stationary decay observed in testing. It seemed “more faithful than a norm, cheaper than the exact $d\times d$ scan,” so it was made the default.
  4. The error: defaulting to a self-invented score instead of first verifying against the paper. Once page 18 + Appendix B parsed cleanly, eq 29 confirmed the faithful approximation is exactly $\|h_t\|^2$, revealing logdet_quantile as neither the exact form nor the paper's approximation — just an unsanctioned intermediate. Configs were then switched to l2_quantile and the true exact mode was implemented.

The correct order — followed only after the fact — is: parse the paper → implement the exact form and the paper's stated approximation → add cheaper variants only as explicit ablations. The saving grace is that it did not change the conclusion (all three score forms tie at ~1.00).
